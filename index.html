Where should you aim?

Treble 20 is the highest-scoring bed on the board, but it is surrounded by the 1 and the 5. If your darts spread widely, aiming at it costs you more in misses than it earns in trebles. This app works out, for any throwing spread, the aim point that gives the highest average score per dart.

The model

Each dart lands at the point you aim at, plus a random error that follows a two-dimensional normal (Gaussian) distribution:

landing = aim + bias + error,   error ~ N(0, Σ),   Σ = [[σx², ρσxσy], [ρσxσy, σy²]]

  • σx and σy are the standard deviations of your left–right and up–down errors, in millimetres. About 68% of darts land within one σ of the centre along each axis.
  • ρ tilts the spread: with ρ > 0 darts that go right also tend to go high.
  • bias is your average miss. If your darts land 10 mm low on average, you should aim 10 mm higher than the spot you want to hit.

The heatmap

Every pixel of the Explore heatmap shows the expected score of one dart when you aim there: the score of every point of the board, weighted by the probability that your dart lands there. That is the board's score function convolved with your Gaussian, computed exactly on a 1 mm grid with a fast Fourier transform. The star marks the best pixel, and 200,000 simulated throws at that point confirm the value.

Measuring your own spread

  1. On the Measure tab, pick a target (the bull is a good choice: it is easy to aim at consistently).
  2. Throw at it 30 or more times. After each throw, or each visit, click where the darts landed.
  3. The app fits your bias, σx, σy and ρ, and shows the best aim point for you. With fewer than 20 darts the estimates are rough.

Your darts are saved in this browser only. Use Save CSV to keep them, or to load them into the original Python script.

Board dimensions

Regulation steel-tip board: bullseye radius 6.35 mm, outer bull 15.9 mm, treble ring 99–107 mm, double ring 162–170 mm. Wire thickness is ignored.

Some results

For a round spread (σx = σy = σ) and no bias:

  • σ up to about 17 mm: treble 20, drifting slightly towards the 5 as σ grows.
  • About 18 to 30 mm: the treble 19 area. Its neighbours, 7 and 16, are much kinder than the 20's neighbours, 1 and 5.
  • Wider than that: the aim slides inwards towards the 7 and 16, and for very wide spreads ends up just lower-left of the bull, so that misses still land on the board.

A spread that is tall rather than wide (good line, bad length) keeps treble 20 worthwhile for much longer: the 20 bed is long and narrow.
